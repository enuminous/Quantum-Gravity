## Abstract

This note defines a low-energy benchmark action coupling conventional Einstein gravity and the Standard Model to an additional canonical scalar. It derives the scalar field equation, stress-energy, flat-space sinusoidal solutions, and the associated pressure frequency, and connects them to the proposed EFMW–Rahasya pressure cycle without equating the scalar's stress-energy pressure with an actual hydrodynamic substrate pressure. A homogeneous Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) truncation supplies a consistent classical gravitational backreaction test. A separate 888-dimensional unitary lattice toy demonstrates what finite-state quantum unitarity can establish—and what it cannot. We end with the actual proof obligations for a complete quantum theory of spacetime, including spacetime emergence, gravitons, Lorentz symmetry, chirality, anomaly cancellation, unitarity, and a nonperturbative or ultraviolet definition.

## 2. Action and conventions

Use natural units $\hbar=c=1$, metric signature $(-+++)$, reduced Planck mass $M_\mathrm{Pl}^2=(8\pi G)^{-1}$, and a canonical real scalar field $\varphi$. A conservative low-energy baseline is

$$
\boxed{
S_0=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{M_\mathrm{Pl}^2}{2}R-\Lambda_\mathrm{vac}
+\mathcal L_\mathrm{SM}(g,\mathrm{SM\ fields})
-\frac12 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\varphi\partial_\nu\varphi
-\frac12 m^2\varphi^2
\right].}
\tag{1}
$$

Here $\Lambda_\mathrm{vac}$ has dimensions of **energy density** (mass$^4$ in natural units), rather than the usual curvature-valued cosmological constant $\Lambda_\mathrm{geom}=\Lambda_\mathrm{vac}/M_\mathrm{Pl}^2$. The SM symbol denotes the standard gauge, fermion, Yukawa and Higgs Lagrangian, coupled to the metric. The scalar is added as an *independent hypothesized sector*; it is **not** claimed to be the Higgs or a known particle.

The Standard Model may be written schematically, with conventional notation, as

$$
\mathcal L_\mathrm{SM}= -\frac14 G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}
-\frac14W^I_{\mu\nu}W^{I\mu\nu}-\frac14B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}
+\sum_f\bar f i\gamma^\mu D_\mu f
+(D_\mu H)^\dagger D^\mu H -V(H)+\mathcal L_\mathrm{Yukawa}
$$

with curved-space vierbein/spin-connection structure for spinors understood. This is a schematic field-content summary, **not** an implementation of all SM quantum interactions or a complete curved-space treatment of spinors.

Possible corrections at energies below a cutoff $\Lambda_*$ can be organized systematically. **A dimensionally consistent illustrative selection** is

$$
S_{\rm EFT}=S_0+\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
 c_1R^2+c_2R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
 +\mu_{\varphi R}\varphi R
 +\lambda_{\varphi H}\varphi^2 H^\dagger H
 +\frac{c_3}{\Lambda_*^2}(\nabla_\mu\varphi\nabla^\mu\varphi)R
 +\cdots\right].
\tag{2}
$$

Here $c_1,c_2,c_3,\lambda_{\varphi H}$ are dimensionless in four dimensions and $\mu_{\varphi R}$ has mass dimension one. Higher-derivative coefficients, counterterms, symmetries and the EFT truncation order would need a full specification before loop calculations. This is **illustrative operator bookkeeping only**, not a measured or fitted correction. The new scalar-sector couplings are not predicted by EFMW.

**For all executable tests we set every additional EFT coefficient to zero** and include no direct scalar–SM interaction.

## 3. Euler–Lagrange equations and Bianchi identity

Varying Eq. (1) with respect to $\varphi$ yields

$$
\boxed{(\Box_g-m^2)\varphi=0,\qquad \Box_g=\nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu.}
\tag{3}
$$

The canonical scalar stress tensor is

$$
\boxed{T^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\varphi\partial_\nu\varphi-g_{\mu\nu}\left[
\frac12\partial_\alpha\varphi\partial^\alpha\varphi+\frac12m^2\varphi^2\right].}
\tag{4}
$$

Varying the metric yields

$$
\boxed{M_\mathrm{Pl}^2G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda_\mathrm{vac}g_{\mu\nu}
=T^{\mathrm{SM}}_{\mu\nu}+T^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\nu}.}
\tag{5}
$$

For solutions of the scalar equation, one has

$$
\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{(\varphi)}=(\Box_g\varphi-m^2\varphi)\partial_\nu\varphi=0.
\tag{6}
$$

The contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}=0$ is consequently consistent with **total** stress-energy conservation, provided the SM sector also satisfies its equations of motion. This does **not** prove that an independently proposed EFMW informational stress tensor is conserved; that remains the separate frozen EFMW ME-007-BIANCHI obligation.

## 4. Homogeneous Minkowski solution and pressure-frequency check

In an externally fixed flat metric $ds^2=-dt^2+d\mathbf x^2$ and for $\varphi(t)$ only, Eq. (3) becomes

$$
\ddot\varphi+m^2\varphi=0,\qquad \varphi(t)=A\cos(mt+\delta).
\tag{7}
$$

**This is a decoupling/test-field approximation**: a homogeneous nonzero oscillating field has nonzero stress-energy and hence does not generally coexist with *exact* Minkowski geometry as a solution of the coupled Einstein system at finite $G$. Set gravitational backreaction aside, take a suitable $G\to0$ limit, or move to the FLRW equations below.

The scalar's homogeneous energy density and isotropic pressure derived from Eq. (4) are

$$
\rho_\varphi=\frac12\dot\varphi^2+\frac12m^2\varphi^2,\qquad
p_\varphi=\frac12\dot\varphi^2-\frac12m^2\varphi^2.
\tag{8}
$$

Using Eq. (7),

$$
\boxed{\rho_\varphi=\frac12m^2A^2,\qquad
p_\varphi=-\frac12m^2A^2\cos(2mt+2\delta).}
\tag{9}
$$

**Conditional frequency result:** the pressure derived from canonical scalar stress-energy oscillates at **twice** the scalar's frequency. If, solely as an illustrative hypothesis, the *pressure* period is identified with Rahasya's proposed Stoney time $t_S$, then the flat free-scalar angular frequency would satisfy $m=\pi/t_S$ (in suitable units with $\hbar=1$), while the field period is $2t_S$. This is a parameter identification, **not** a derived universal clock. The statement is model-specific: it need not hold for other potentials, matter constitutive laws, expansion, noncanonical kinetic terms, or Rahasya's actual hypothesized substrate.

Compare RHC-001's *independently assumed* linear pressure map

$$p_\mathrm{linear}=p_0+\chi u,\tag{10}$$

where the source field $u$ is not automatically normalized as canonical $\varphi$. The pressure maps (9) and (10) are inequivalent. We must not silently identify them.

## 5. Fully coupled classical FLRW truncation

Use a spatially flat FLRW metric, $ds^2=-dt^2+a(t)^2d\mathbf x^2$, with $H=\dot a/a$. For a homogeneous scalar with constant vacuum energy, no SM matter or radiation in the *numerical truncation*,

$$
\boxed{\ddot\varphi+3H\dot\varphi+m^2\varphi=0,}
\tag{11}
$$

$$
\boxed{3M_\mathrm{Pl}^2H^2=\frac12\dot\varphi^2+\frac12m^2\varphi^2+\Lambda_\mathrm{vac},}
\tag{12}
$$

$$
\boxed{\dot H=-\frac{\dot\varphi^2}{2M_\mathrm{Pl}^2}.}
\tag{13}
$$

The scalar continuity equation follows:

$$
\boxed{\dot\rho_\varphi+3H(\rho_\varphi+p_\varphi)=0.}
\tag{14}
$$

The script integrates $\varphi$, $\dot\varphi$, $H$ and $\ln a$ with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, then **independently compares** the integrated $H$ against the Friedmann constraint Eq. (12). The numerical residual, rather than a trivially enforced identity, is reported. All output values are **dimensionless rescaled toy units**; the scalar is not calibrated as a cosmic medium.

Under expansion, the exact sinusoid and constant scalar energy of Eq. (9) no longer hold; damping shifts the oscillator amplitude and modifies the pressure cycle.

## 7. What low-energy quantized gravity means

Expanding about a specified classical background,

$$
 g_{\mu\nu}=\bar g_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu},\qquad
 \kappa=\sqrt{32\pi G},
 \tag{15}
$$

and properly gauge-fixing the diffeomorphism redundancy permits perturbative calculations involving graviton fluctuations. Gravitational EFT produces controlled low-energy quantum corrections while **not claiming ultraviolet completeness**. See Donoghue, *Phys. Rev. D* **50**, 3874 (1994), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.50.3874](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.50.3874).

**Not done in this archive:** graviton quantization, path integral, Faddeev–Popov ghosts, BRST consistency, renormalization, amplitudes, loop corrections, or derivation of a UV limit.

## 8. Base888: microscopic emergence is the missing theorem

A simple candidate finite quantum kinematics is

$$
\mathcal H_\Gamma=\bigotimes_{v\in\Gamma}\mathbb C^{888}.
\tag{16}
$$

It requires a choice of graph $\Gamma$, self-adjoint Hamiltonian $H_\Gamma$, a physical constraint/gauge structure, and a family of coarse-graining maps. The number **888**, by itself, has no demonstrated relationship to quantum gravity or four-dimensional geometry.

The included `base888_toy.py` uses a **single 888-level cyclic graph** with a Hermitian nearest-neighbor hopping Hamiltonian,

$$H_\mathrm{ring}=J\sum_{j=0}^{887}(|j\rangle\langle j+1|+|j+1\rangle\langle j|),$$

## 9. Completion criteria for a quantum theory of spacetime

1. **Microscopic specification:** define exact local Hilbert spaces, Hamiltonian/transition laws, graph dynamics and constraints. Prove unitarity or an appropriate completely positive open-system formulation.
2. **Geometric emergence:** define distances, areas, volumes, causal order and the relational observables used to read them; prove a controlled classical $3+1$ Lorentzian limit.
3. **Graviton sector:** derive physical gapless spin-2 modes with two helicities, correct interactions and equivalence-principle behavior. Handle background dependence and gauge constraints.
4. **Standard Model content:** derive the *actual* chiral gauge structure, anomaly cancellation, matter representations, mass/generation structure and Higgs couplings without silently inserting them.
5. **Quantum consistency:** establish an ultraviolet definition or controlled completion, unitarity, locality/causality as applicable, and well-defined physical observables.
6. **Experimental separation:** calculate independently checkable deviations or constraints and compare against conventional EFT/GR+SM baselines. Use preregistered failures and null tests.
